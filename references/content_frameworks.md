# Content Frameworks and Templates

Choose a framework based on reader intent. Frameworks are structures, not formulas that should be forced into every topic.

## 1. Audience-first brief

Before drafting, define:
- **Audience:** Who is reading?
- **Situation:** What prompted the search or decision?
- **Need:** What question must be answered?
- **Promise:** What will the reader know or be able to do?
- **Evidence:** What research, examples, or data support the answer?
- **Next step:** What action is useful after reading?

## 2. Common message frameworks

### Problem, Agitation, Solution (PAS)

Useful when the audience already recognizes a problem.

1. Describe the problem precisely.
2. Explain its real consequence without fearmongering.
3. Present a credible solution and its trade-offs.

### Attention, Interest, Desire, Action (AIDA)

Useful for a focused promotional message.

1. Attention: earn attention with a relevant opening.
2. Interest: explain what matters to the audience.
3. Desire: show benefits with evidence or a concrete example.
4. Action: give one clear next step.

### Before, After, Bridge (BAB)

Useful for explaining a change.

1. Before: describe the current state.
2. After: show a realistic improved state.
3. Bridge: explain the steps connecting them.

### Feature, Advantage, Benefit (FAB)

Useful for product education.

- Feature: what the product contains or does.
- Advantage: how that capability differs.
- Benefit: why the difference matters to this audience.

Do not present a feature as a benefit without explaining the connection.

## 3. Blog article template

~~~markdown
# [Clear title that states the reader's outcome]

[Opening: audience problem, context, and promise.]

## What you need to know

[Concise definition or summary.]

## Why it matters

[Impact, constraints, and evidence.]

## Step 1: [Action]

[Explanation, example, and potential pitfall.]

## Step 2: [Action]

[Explanation, example, and potential pitfall.]

## Common mistakes

[Specific mistakes and how to avoid them.]

## Practical checklist

[Actions readers can apply.]

## Conclusion

[Key takeaway and realistic next step.]
~~~

## 4. How-to guide template

- State the expected end result.
- List prerequisites and assumptions.
- Number the steps in execution order.
- Include expected output after important steps.
- Explain common errors and recovery.
- Identify where the procedure may vary by environment.
- End with a verification checklist.

## 5. Case study template

1. Context: organization, audience, and starting state.
2. Challenge: the specific problem.
3. Constraints: time, budget, technology, or policy.
4. Approach: what was actually done.
5. Evidence: measurable results and measurement period.
6. Limitations: confounding factors and what cannot be concluded.
7. Lessons: what another team can reasonably apply.

Never invent customer names, quotes, metrics, or outcomes.

## 6. Landing page template

1. Headline focused on the audience outcome.
2. Subheadline that explains the product and audience.
3. Primary call to action.
4. The problem and its consequences.
5. How the offer works.
6. Benefits supported by proof.
7. Use cases or audience segments.
8. Objections and honest limitations.
9. Relevant testimonials or case evidence with permission.
10. Final call to action.

## 7. Email template

- Subject: specific and relevant, not deceptive.
- Preview: adds useful context.
- Opening: establish relevance quickly.
- Main body: one central idea.
- Proof: example, data, or explanation.
- CTA: one clear action.
- Footer: required identity, preference, and compliance details.

## 8. Content repurposing matrix

| Source asset | Adaptation | Possible output |
|---|---|---|
| Long-form guide | Extract key steps | Short educational carousel |
| Research report | Explain one finding | Data-led social post |
| Webinar | Select useful segments | Short video clips |
| Case study | Focus on one lesson | Founder or team post |
| Product tutorial | Break into tasks | Short how-to series |
| FAQ collection | Answer one question at a time | Search-focused article |

Repurposing means adapting the content to the destination, not copying the same text unchanged.

## 9. Editorial quality gate

Before publishing:
- verify every factual claim;
- attribute quotations and data;
- check dates, product names, and links;
- remove repetitive or unsupported claims;
- confirm the draft meets the reader's intent;
- review accessibility, structure, and mobile readability;
- confirm the call to action matches the page objective.
