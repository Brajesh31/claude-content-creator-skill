# Brand Voice Guidelines

Use this guide to define a repeatable voice. A brand voice is the stable personality of the communication; tone is how that voice adapts to a particular situation.

## 2. Voice attributes

Choose three to five attributes. Define both the desired behavior and the behavior to avoid.

| Attribute | We sound like | We do not sound like |
|---|---|---|
| Clear | Direct, specific, easy to understand | Vague or jargon-heavy |
| Knowledgeable | Evidence-led and precise | Arrogant or needlessly technical |
| Human | Natural, respectful, empathetic | Forced, robotic, or overfamiliar |
| Confident | Decisive when evidence is strong | Exaggerated or absolute |
| Helpful | Practical and audience-focused | Pushy or self-congratulatory |

Replace these defaults with attributes that reflect the actual brand.

## 3. Brand archetypes as a writing aid

Archetypes are optional creative shorthand. They are not a substitute for audience research.

| Archetype | Communication tendency | Common risk |
|---|---|---|
| Sage | Explains, teaches, clarifies | Can become academic |
| Explorer | Encourages discovery and possibility | Can become unfocused |
| Creator | Values originality and craft | Can overvalue novelty |
| Hero | Emphasizes progress and achievement | Can sound aggressive |
| Caregiver | Reassures and supports | Can become paternalistic |
| Everyperson | Uses accessible, relatable language | Can become generic |
| Jester | Uses wit and levity | Can undermine serious topics |
| Ruler | Communicates authority and order | Can sound controlling |
| Magician | Frames transformation and outcomes | Can overpromise |
| Lover | Emphasizes connection and experience | Can sound sentimental |
| Outlaw | Challenges conventional thinking | Can provoke without purpose |
| Innocent | Values optimism and simplicity | Can ignore complexity |

Select one primary archetype and, at most, one secondary influence. Document examples that demonstrate the blend.

## 4. Tone controls

Adjust these dimensions to the audience and situation.

| Dimension | Lower setting | Higher setting |
|---|---|---|
| Formality | Conversational | Formal |
| Energy | Calm | Expressive |
| Technical depth | Plain-language | Specialist |
| Humor | Serious | Playful |
| Directness | Diplomatic | Blunt |
| Emotional warmth | Neutral | Empathetic |

A crisis update, technical guide, sales page, and social post may use different tone settings while retaining the same brand voice.

## 5. Language rules

### Prefer
- Specific verbs and concrete nouns.
- Short sentences when explaining complex ideas.
- Active voice when the actor matters.
- Evidence, examples, and clear caveats.
- Inclusive language and accessible explanations.
- One main message and one primary call to action.

### Avoid
- Unsupported superlatives and guaranteed outcomes.
- Empty phrases such as "revolutionary" or "world-class" without proof.
- Fake urgency and manipulative scarcity.
- Excessive exclamation marks and emoji.
- Unexplained acronyms or internal jargon.
- Competitor claims that cannot be substantiated.

## 8. Review checklist

- [ ] Does the content address a real audience need?
- [ ] Are the chosen voice attributes recognizable?
- [ ] Is the tone appropriate to the situation?
- [ ] Are product and technical claims accurate?
- [ ] Is the language concrete and readable?

- [ ] Does the content avoid stereotypes and manipulative language?
